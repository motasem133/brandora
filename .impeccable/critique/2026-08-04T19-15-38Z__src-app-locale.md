## Design Specificity Verdict

**Split down the middle, and the seam runs along a directory boundary.**

Three surfaces could not be lifted into another product. `brand-specimen.tsx` and `identity-plate.tsx` make the card *be* the brand's ground — its paper, its heading face, its palette as the sheet's base edge, a ring mixed from its own ink. `logo-studio.tsx` + `logo-composer.tsx` stage the mark on the brand's own canvas with a composer that requires Cmd/Ctrl+Enter *because* one keystroke spends six credits. The landing's `#kit` section argues by exhibit — real Makān wordmarks, the real hex codes, the real published site.

Three surfaces are the admin template DESIGN.md was written to escape. `/dashboard/billing` opens with a three-tile stat row and continues into four same-size plan cards. All five `/admin/*` routes open with four `<Stat>` cards in `lg:grid-cols-4` — the literal anti-reference, unmodified. The builder is Webflow-lite: no project name, no brand mark, nothing that says this site was generated from a brief.

And `/en/pricing` is a *third* language — different radii, different bullets (`· ` versus check icons), different type, describing the same four plans **from a different data source** (`landing-data.ts` versus the `plans` table), so the two pages can disagree about what a plan includes.

The audit's verdict names the mechanism: **two systems split by directory.** `ui/` and `features/projects/` express the product-specific system; `builder/`, `admin/`, `onboarding/` and `app/[locale]/page.tsx` express generic Tailwind.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over `src/app src/components`: **8 findings, exit 2 — 6 real, 2 verified false positives** (`globals.css:482` is `calc(var(--radius-card) - 1px)` with the regex catching the calc tail; `logo.tsx:6` matched the literal `<img>` inside a JSDoc explaining why the component uses inline SVG *instead* of one). The real six are three off-palette zinc greys and an off-scale radius and font-size in `global-error.tsx` — structurally forced to inline its styles since it replaces the root layout, but off-palette values regardless — plus `icon-chip`'s `0.75rem` radius in `globals.css:442`.

The detector's blind spot is the story: it scans literal hex, so raw Tailwind palette classes pass straight through. Four exist, all in `builder.tsx` — including `ring-violet-500`, a second violet sitting beside Signal Violet, exactly what the One Ramp Rule forbids.

**No user-visible overlay.** Assessment B ran measured browser evidence — 38 full-page screenshots across 19 routes at 1440×900 and 390×844, with scripted contrast, touch-target, heading, accessible-name and overflow probes — rather than the injected on-page overlay. Nothing is highlighted in your browser.

## Overall Impression

The parts of this product that face a customer's brand are genuinely good, and they are good on purpose: the guest-palette runtime theme, the composer that guards a six-credit keystroke, the contact-sheet dashboard that refuses stat tiles. The parts that face the *product's own* operations — billing, admin, the builder, the marketing page — read as if a different team shipped them against a different system.

The single biggest opportunity is not a new visual direction. It is that **DESIGN.md is right and the implementation drifted from it**, measurably: 56 off-scale radii versus 43 on-scale, a `Textarea` primitive imported nowhere while five hand-rolled textareas exist, and seven patterns (Select, Tabs, Table, EmptyState, Skeleton, Switch, Tooltip) re-invented 2–6 times each — each time without the accessibility attributes the primitive would have carried once. That is how drift becomes WCAG debt, and it is why the a11y findings cluster exactly where the drift is.

## What's Working

1. **The guest-palette runtime theme** (`src/lib/brand-theme.ts` → `theme.vars`). A server component hands its whole subtree to another brand's colours through CSS variables, contrast-checked at runtime with real WCAG relative luminance. `softened()` documents the arithmetic proof that a flat 42% `color-mix` *cannot* reach 4.5:1 for any colour pair, and steps the mix until it does. This is the product's thesis made structural.
2. **RTL is the best-executed dimension in the codebase.** 71 logical-property utilities against effectively zero mirroring physical ones, 15 `rtl:` glyph variants, a separate Arabic stack at 1.85 leading, and `dir="auto"` on user content with a comment explaining why `lang` alone doesn't fix bidi. Most codebases claiming RTL support don't reach this.
3. **Two primitives built on the platform rather than around it.** `ConfirmDialog` uses native `<dialog>` + `showModal()`, so focus trap, Escape, focus restoration and background inertness are the browser's; `Disclosure` uses native `<details>`, so it survives find-in-page with no JS. Zero findings against either, from any of the three passes.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] There is no navigation below `lg` — Billing and Settings are unreachable on a phone
**Why it matters:** measured at 390×844, the complete set of visible links on `/en/dashboard` is the wordmark, Onboarding, and one project. `sidebar.tsx` is `hidden … lg:block` and the topbar adds no drawer, sheet or bottom bar. A customer cannot top up credits, change plan, verify their email or reach settings from a phone; the primary CTA collapses to an unlabelled `+`. DESIGN.md already prescribes the fix — *"Below `lg` the side rail becomes a horizontal scrolling row"* — it was written and never implemented.
**Fix:** render the four `NAV` entries as a bottom bar or a topbar sheet below `lg`; restore the "New project" label.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P0] The builder cannot be operated by keyboard, and loses its tools below 1280px
**Why it matters:** `builder.tsx:315` selects a block with a bare `<div onClick>` — no role, no `tabIndex`, no key handler — and selection is the only route to the inspector, so a keyboard or switch user cannot edit any website content at all (WCAG 2.1.1, 4.1.2, both Level A). The block library is `hidden … lg:block` and the inspector `hidden … xl:block` with no fallback, so below 1280px the builder renders a canvas you can look at and nothing you can do — while Reorder and Delete still render, so blocks can be destroyed but not created. The page also has no `h1` (its only headings are the fixture site's three "About us" blocks) and logs the app's sole console error on every load: a dnd-kit hydration mismatch from `<DndContext>` with no `id` prop.
**Fix:** make block selection a real button with `aria-pressed`; collapse library and inspector into a sheet or tab strip below `xl`; add a builder header carrying the brand's name, mark and a link back to the project; pass `id` to `DndContext`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] `/ar` marketing is untranslated English mirrored into RTL
**Why it matters:** `app/[locale]/page.tsx` calls `setRequestLocale(locale)` and then hardcodes every string in English, so the bidi algorithm reorders it: the H1 renders **"brand entire Your ,identity"** and the deck **".generated in one run"** with the full stop jumped to the visual left. Assessment B confirms `/ar` serves an identical heading tree and identical contrast failures to `/en`. This is the front door for a product whose second-largest claim is *"Arabic is generated natively, not translated"* — and the app behind the login is properly localised, so the one page that must prove the claim is the only one that doesn't. `/ar/pricing` compounds it by mixing Arabic-Indic numerals in feature lists with Western numerals in prices, inside one card.
**Fix:** move `page.tsx` and `landing-data.ts` copy into `messages/{en,ar}.json`; verify the H1 word-split in RTL; settle one numeral system per locale.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] Guest brand palettes silently repaint Brandora's own chrome
**Why it matters:** `brand-theme.ts:183` re-binds the **global** token names `--brand-primary` / `--brand-secondary` inside every brand scope, so everything downstream switches to the customer's colour — including `globals.css:157`'s `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--brand-primary) }`. A brand with a pale-cream primary on cream paper gets an *invisible keyboard focus ring* on its dashboard card (`brand-specimen.tsx:74`) and on its logo concepts (`logo-studio.tsx:136`). Related: `--brand-accent` is gated at 3:1 and then used for the slogan at 18–20px regular, where AA requires 4.5:1, and `--brand-primary`/`--brand-secondary` pass through with no contrast check at all. WCAG 1.4.11, 2.4.11, 1.4.3. The rest of that file is careful and correct — these are the holes in an otherwise good guard.
**Fix:** namespace the guest tokens (`--guest-primary`), so chrome tokens can never be shadowed by untrusted generated input; gate `--brand-accent` at body contrast or split it into body and large variants.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The form-error contract was built and never finished — 23 sites, zero associations
**Why it matters:** `FieldError` renders a bare `<p>` with no `id` and no `role`, so nothing can point at it. Repo-wide: **0** `aria-describedby` on form fields, **0** `role="alert"`, and `aria-invalid` on 6 of 35 fields. A screen-reader user who submits an invalid form hears nothing change, and focusing the field tells them nothing. WCAG 3.3.1 and 1.3.1 (A), 4.1.3 (AA). The correct pattern already exists in the repo at `register-form.tsx:82-140` — it needs one more attribute.
**Fix:** give `FieldError` an `id` and `role="alert"`; wire `aria-describedby` + `aria-invalid` from every field through a shared field hook. One primitive change, then mechanical.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Billing and admin reintroduce both banned patterns; pricing exists twice, from two sources
**Why it matters:** DESIGN.md names them as regressions "regardless of how it looks in isolation" — and `/dashboard/billing` opens with a three-tile stat row above four same-size plan cards carrying **two** competing gradient CTAs against the documented "normally one per view", on the page that handles money. Every `/admin/*` route opens with four `<Stat>` cards. Meanwhile `/en/pricing` renders the same four plans as the landing page in a different visual system with a different feature list from a different data source, so a visitor comparing them sees two products.
**Fix:** give billing one lead statement (plan, renewal, balance) as meta the way the dashboard already does, then an asymmetric plan ladder; rebuild the admin overview around its by-plan table with the four figures as caption; make `/en/pricing` render the landing's pricing component from the `plans` table, or delete it.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] The peak of the entire product is delivered as a component unmounting
**Why it matters:** the run finishes, `done` fires, and `generation-panel.tsx` returns `null`. The one moment the whole arc is built toward — a brand existing that did not exist ninety seconds ago — has no transition, no naming of what arrived, no scroll to the plate. Before it, the wizard ends on "Create project" and the project page then asks a *second* time with "Generate your brand": eight questions answered and the thing still hasn't started. The product spends 168 lines on pointer-velocity throw physics for a card hover and zero on the ninety seconds that justify the purchase.
**Fix:** replace the null return with a completion state that names the sections produced, scrolls the identity plate into view, then retires; relabel the wizard's terminal button to the act it performs and put the price and balance on it.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable delight`

### [P2] Twelve landing text nodes below AA, and the whole page is invisible without JavaScript
**Why it matters:** measured, not estimated — `text-white/35` at 3.11–3.15:1, `text-white/40` at 3.73–3.82:1, `text-white/45` at 4.49:1 (a hairline miss that is still a fail), all at body size. And with JavaScript disabled, **49 elements stay at `opacity: 0` permanently** at both widths: roughly 4,000px of blank ground below the hero, because the reveal never fires. Separately, light theme fails on every status badge — `Active`/`Verified` at **2.00:1**, `Unverified` at **1.84:1**, billing's `+490` at **2.28:1** — all passing in dark, so the failure is light-theme-only across billing, settings and admin.
**Fix:** floor the opacity ramp at `/50` and delete `/35` and `/40` from the vocabulary; make reveals additive over server-rendered visible content; give the badge tones light-theme values.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Minor Observations

- `src/components/ui/label.tsx` is `text-sm font-medium`; DESIGN.md's `label` token is 12px/600/`0.12em`/uppercase. The primitive named Label doesn't implement the token named label, so the uppercase eyebrow is hand-rolled in at least four feature files.
- `--brand-ink-soft` and `--brand-ink-faint` call `softened()` with identical arguments and resolve to the same string — two tokens, one value, so every "quieter" tier renders at the same weight.
- The landing's flagship "Four logo concepts" tile overflows its card by 179px at 1440px: two of the four Makān concepts are covered by the tile below. The one place a visitor sees real output shows half of it.
- `Aurora`, `LogoTile` and `ParallaxFrame` in `motion-hero.tsx` have zero imports — dead code carrying `blur-[130px]` infinite animations.
- `pricing/page.tsx` renders features as `<li>· {feature}</li>`, a literal middle dot, where the landing uses check icons.
- Light-theme `--text-muted` on `--bg-elevated` measures 4.47:1 — one token, one surface, 0.03 short.
- `copy-button.tsx` documents a *deliberate* decision not to use `aria-live` because twenty instances would be noise. That reasoning is right; it just was never extended to the four places that genuinely need a live region.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the darkroom's whole argument is that the chrome withdraws so the generated brand can be judged — why does the moment the brand first exists have no staging at all? What would it cost to make the completion of a run the loudest thing in the product?
2. PRODUCT.md says the account that matters is the freelancer running identity after identity, but every default is tuned for the founder who runs once. Which single surface would you rebuild first for run number ten rather than run number one?
3. `/en/pricing` and the landing's pricing section describe the same four plans from two data sources, in two visual languages. Which is the truth — and does the second need to exist?
4. Publishing puts a customer's site on the open internet with less confirmation than deleting a draft. Is that because it's genuinely reversible, or because nobody has yet pictured a customer publishing "About us / About us / About us" under their client's name — which is what the demo site currently contains?
5. Two systems split by directory: `ui/` + `features/projects/` versus `builder/` + `admin/` + the landing page. Is the second set worth bringing up to the first, or worth deleting and regenerating against it?
